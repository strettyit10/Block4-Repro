1Hierarchical levels of sexual differentiation

The Gist Sex is not one decision — it is a cascade in which each tier specifies the next: chromosomal → gonadal → hormonal → morphological → secondary sex characteristics → brain & behavior. The whole system is built around one rule: the default phenotype is female. Male-typical anatomy only appears when the fetal testis actively secretes hormones to override the default. When any step fails, the tiers decouple and you see intersex phenotypes on the boards.

1 · Chromosomal sex & SRY

The switch gene
  • SRY = Sex-determining Region of the Y chromosome:
    • Encoded on the short arm of Y (Yp).FA 2024 Repro
    • Encodes the testis-determining factor (TDF), a transcription factor that activates SOX9.
    • SOX9 pushes the bipotential germinal ridge toward testis fate (Sertoli-cell commitment).
  • Without SRY:
    • Germinal ridge adopts the default ovarian pathway (WNT4, RSPO1, FOXL2).
    • This is why the default is female at every downstream tier.
Classic karyotype variants
  • 45,X (Turner):
    • One X missing → streak gonads → no hormones.Pathoma Ch 15
    • Female phenotype, short stature, webbed neck, bicuspid aortic valve.
  • 47,XXY (Klinefelter):
    • SRY present → male phenotype; extra X impairs seminiferous tubules → infertility + tall stature.
  • 46,XX SRY+ (de la Chapelle): Y→X translocation of SRY → male phenotype.
  • 46,XY SRY− (Swyer): loss-of-function SRY → streak gonads, female phenotype. Must remove Y-bearing gonads (gonadoblastoma risk).

2 · Gonadal differentiation — the bipotential germinal ridge

Shared starting point
  • Week-5 germinal ridge:
    • Mesodermal tissue on the medial side of the mesonephros (proto-kidney).Costanzo 6e ch 10
    • Equally capable of becoming ovary or testis — a true bipotential primordium.
  • The two fates:
    • Testis: SRY → SOX9 → Sertoli cells form first; Leydig cells second.
    • Ovary: default; granulosa + theca cells form around primordial germ cells.
Timing matters
  • Sertoli first, then Leydig:
    • Sertoli cells secrete AMH early → Müllerian ducts begin regressing before testosterone peaks.
    • Leydig cells secrete T slightly later → Wolffian ducts stabilize.
    • This order explains why a Sertoli-only defect can leave a 46,XY with a persistent uterus (LO 6).

3 · Hormonal tier — T vs AMH vs DHT

Who does what
  • Testosterone (fetal Leydig):
    • Stabilizes Wolffian ducts into epididymis, vas deferens, seminal vesicles, ejaculatory duct.
    • Acts paracrine — each testis masculinizes its ipsilateral duct.
    • Drives male brain patterning after local aromatization to estradiol.
  • AMH (fetal Sertoli):
    • Regresses Müllerian ducts → no uterus, tubes, or upper vagina in males.
    • Loss → persistent Müllerian duct syndrome (46,XY with uterus at hernia repair).
  • DHT (peripheral, via 5α-reductase type 2):
    • Virilizes external genitalia (penis, scrotum), prostate, and pattern hair.
    • Loss → normal internal male + female-appearing external at birth (\"guevedoces\" phenotype at puberty).

4 · Morphological tier (hand-off to LO 2)

Default is female
  • Müllerian vs Wolffian:
    • Both duct systems exist side-by-side at week 6. Survival depends on what hormones reach them.
    • Default (no hormones): Müllerian persists, Wolffian regresses → female internal anatomy.
    • With T + AMH: Wolffian persists, Müllerian regresses → male internal anatomy.

5 · Secondary sex & brain-behavior tier

Secondary sex (puberty)
  • Driven by gonadal & adrenal steroids once HPG axis reactivates:
    • Females: breast development (E2), gluteofemoral fat (E2), pubic/axillary hair (adrenal DHEA → androgens).
    • Males: facial/body hair (DHT), muscle mass (T), laryngeal growth / voice deepening (T), androgenic alopecia (DHT).
    • Estrogen closes epiphyses in BOTH sexes — aromatase-deficient patients (XX or XY) grow extremely tall.
Brain & behavior — organizational vs activational
  • Organizational (perinatal, permanent):
    • Androgen → local aromatization to estradiol in hypothalamus → apoptosis of AVPV kisspeptin neurons in males.
    • Consequence: males retain negative feedback only; females keep both negative and positive → females can mount an LH surge, males cannot.
  • Activational (adult, reversible):
    • Libido tracks current T/E2. Mood and migraine pattern track menstrual E2. Postpartum drop in placental steroids can precipitate mood disorders.
  • Aromatase paradox:
    • T masculinizes the male brain only after local conversion to estradiol. Fetal α-fetoprotein binds maternal/fetal E2 and keeps it out of the female fetal brain, so female brain is protected.

6 · When the cascade breaks — intersex & EDCs

Each broken tier makes its own vignette
  • Break types & results:
    • Chromosomal (Turner 45,X): streak ovary → no hormones → female-appearing with short stature + cardiac findings.
    • Gonadal (Swyer 46,XY SRY−): no testis → no T + no AMH → female external & internal; Y-gonads need removal.
    • Hormonal excess (21-OH CAH, 46,XX): adrenal androgens in utero → virilized external but normal uterus/ovary.
    • Receptor (AIS 46,XY): testes + T + DHT all there, but AR non-functional → female external, no uterus (AMH still works).
    • Enzyme (5α-RD 46,XY): normal T, low DHT → internal male + female-appearing external at birth; virilizes at puberty.
Endocrine-disrupting compounds (EDCs)
  • Categories & windows:
    • Plasticizers (BPA, phthalates), pesticides (DDT), PCBs, and historic DES exposure can mimic or block sex-steroid signaling.
    • Critical windows: perinatal organizational effects can be lifelong.
    • Rodent 2-M female (flanked by 2 male fetuses) is a classic example of intrauterine androgen exposure altering adult reproductive behavior.
★ HIGH-YIELD for USMLE
  • Default phenotype is female. Female anatomy needs zero hormones in utero; male anatomy requires both T/DHT (masculinization) and AMH (defeminization).
  • SRY — not Y itself — determines sex. 46,XX with SRY translocated onto X is male; 46,XY with mutated SRY is female (Swyer).
  • Sertoli first, Leydig second. AMH must hit the Müllerian ducts before T arrives to stabilize Wolffian ducts.
  • Estrogen closes epiphyses in both sexes. Aromatase or ER mutations → extremely tall stature.
  • Male brain masculinization is actually estradiol. T is aromatized locally in the hypothalamus; α-fetoprotein protects the female fetus.

🏥 CLINICAL PEARL
  • Swyer syndrome (46,XY, SRY mutation) — female phenotype, streak gonads. Y-bearing streak gonads carry gonadoblastoma risk and must be surgically removed.
  • Complete AIS — 46,XY with female external anatomy, testes in inguinal canal, no uterus, primary amenorrhea. Peripheral aromatization of T → E2 gives breast development.
  • 5α-reductase deficiency — \"guevedoces\" or \"penis at 12\": pubertal T surge partially virilizes externally.
  • Aromatase deficiency — unclosed epiphyses in both XX and XY → very tall adults with osteoporosis.

2Hormones required for morphological sex

The Gist Male morphology is built by two independent processes, and neither runs without hormones. Testosterone (fetal Leydig) stabilizes the Wolffian ducts into internal male plumbing. AMH (fetal Sertoli) regresses the Müllerian ducts so there is no uterus. Peripheral DHT (made from T by 5α-reductase type 2) virilizes the external genitalia and prostate. Absence of any of these produces a mix-and-match phenotype — the bread and butter of DSD (disorders of sexual development) vignettes.

📚 Going Deeper — the details behind the gist

1 · The default-is-female rule

What \"no hormones needed\" means
  • Feminization & demasculinization:
    • Default developmental programs — do not require a hormonal signal.
    • This is why an early-gonadectomized fetus of any karyotype develops female anatomy.Costanzo 6e ch 10
  • Masculinization & defeminization:
    • Masculinization requires testosterone (+ DHT peripherally).
    • Defeminization requires AMH.
    • Both must occur for complete male morphology.
Protecting the female fetus
  • α-fetoprotein:
    • Binds maternal and fetal estrogens → keeps them out of the fetal brain and reproductive tract.
    • This is why maternal estrogens don't feminize or masculinize (via aromatization) a female fetus.

2 · Internal ducts — Müllerian vs Wolffian

The two duct systems
  • Müllerian (paramesonephric) ducts:
    • Default: persist → fallopian tubes, uterus, cervix, upper ⅓ of vagina.
    • In males: regressed by AMH.
    • Lower ⅔ of vagina comes from the urogenital sinus (endoderm), NOT Müllerian.
  • Wolffian (mesonephric) ducts:
    • Default: regress in the absence of testosterone.
    • In males: stabilized by T into epididymis, vas deferens, seminal vesicles, ejaculatory duct.
    • T acts paracrine — high local concentration from the adjacent testis is required.FA 2024 Repro; B&B Repro
Timing of AMH and T
  • Weeks 6–7:
    • Sertoli cells form first → AMH secretion begins → Müllerian regression begins.
  • Weeks 8–12:
    • Leydig cells appear → T rises → Wolffian stabilization.
    • This order is why a defect in AMH can leave a male with a uterus (PMDS) but otherwise normal male anatomy.

3 · External genitalia — tubercle, folds, swellings, sinus

The four primordia
  • Genital tubercle:
    • Female (default, no DHT): clitoris.
    • Male (DHT present): glans penis.
  • Urogenital folds:
    • Female: labia minora.
    • Male: penile shaft; fuse in the midline → penile (spongy) urethra. Failure to fuse → hypospadias.
  • Labioscrotal swellings:
    • Female: labia majora.
    • Male: scrotum (midline raphe = fusion scar).
  • Urogenital sinus:
    • Female: lower ⅔ vagina, urethra, Skene & Bartholin glands.
    • Male: prostate, bulbourethral (Cowper) glands, prostatic/membranous urethra.
Why DHT and not T?
  • 5α-reductase type 2:
    • Expressed in genital skin, prostate, hair follicles.
    • Converts T → DHT with much higher AR affinity.
    • Finasteride / dutasteride block this enzyme (BPH, alopecia).Lippincott Pharm 7e
  • T vs DHT territory:
    • T = Tubes (internal Wolffian structures), axial musculature, brain patterning, deep voice.
    • DHT = Dick + Prostate + Pattern hair (external + prostate + scalp/body).

4 · Clinical mutations side-by-side

Complete AIS (46,XY, AR loss-of-function)
  • Fetal state:
    • Testes descend partially (often inguinal). T + DHT produced but cannot signal.
    • Wolffian fails (no T signal). Müllerian regressed (AMH still works). No uterus.
    • External: female-appearing (no DHT effect).
  • Puberty & labs:
    • Breast development from peripheral aromatization of T to E2.
    • No pubic/axillary hair (AR required). Primary amenorrhea.
    • Labs: ↑ T, ↑ LH (no negative feedback), elevated E2.
5α-reductase type 2 deficiency (46,XY)
  • Internal male normal (T + AMH intact).
  • External female-appearing at birth (no DHT in utero).
  • Pubertal T surge partially virilizes externally → \"guevedoces\" / \"penis at 12\".
  • Labs: normal T, elevated T:DHT ratio.
21-OH CAH (46,XX)
  • Can't make cortisol or aldosterone → substrate shunts to androgens (DHEA, androstenedione, T).
  • Ovaries + uterus + upper vagina all normal (no gonadal T, no AMH).
  • External: virilized by androgens in utero → fused labia, clitoromegaly, ambiguous genitalia.
  • Systemic: salt-wasting, hyperkalemia, hypotension; elevated 17-OH-progesterone on newborn screen.Pathoma Ch 15
Persistent Müllerian duct syndrome (46,XY)
  • Loss-of-function of AMH or AMH receptor.
  • T intact → normal external male + Wolffian.
  • AMH failed → persistent uterus and fallopian tubes (often discovered at inguinal hernia repair).
★ HIGH-YIELD for USMLE
  • Female phenotype requires NO hormones. Male phenotype requires BOTH T/DHT AND AMH.
  • Müllerian derivatives = FUTV: Fallopian tubes, Uterus, Top ⅓ of vagina, cerVix. Lower ⅔ vagina = urogenital sinus (endoderm).
  • Wolffian derivatives = SEED: Seminal vesicles, Epididymis, Ejaculatory duct, Ductus (vas) deferens.
  • T for Tubes, DHT for Dick/Prostate/Pattern hair. Memorize this split.
  • High T + no uterus + testes in inguinal canal + female external = Complete AIS.
  • Virilization at puberty after being raised female = 5α-reductase type 2 deficiency.

3HPG axis in females & feedback control

The Gist The female HPG axis is the only endocrine axis that uses both negative and positive feedback from the same hormone — estradiol. Pulsatile GnRH from the hypothalamus drives LH & FSH from the anterior pituitary, which in turn stimulate the ovarian two-cell unit (theca makes androgens under LH; granulosa converts them to estrogens via aromatase under FSH). Estradiol usually suppresses GnRH/LH/FSH (negative feedback). But once estradiol crosses ~200 pg/mL for ~48 h, a second kisspeptin population in the AVPV hypothalamus flips the switch to positive feedback → LH surge → ovulation. Progesterone blocks positive feedback, which is why the luteal phase doesn't produce a second surge.

📚 Going Deeper — the details behind the gist

1 · GnRH → LH/FSH pathway

Hypothalamic origin
  • GnRH neurons:
    • Cell bodies in the medial preoptic area (POA) and arcuate nucleus.Costanzo 6e ch 10
    • Axons project to the median eminence → release GnRH into the hypophyseal portal system.
    • GnRH = 10-amino-acid peptide (decapeptide).
  • Pulse generator:
    • GnRH is released in pulses every 60–90 min.
    • Kisspeptin (arcuate) drives tonic pulses; AVPV kisspeptin drives the pre-ovulatory surge.
Pituitary gonadotropes
  • LH & FSH structure:
    • α + β heterodimer glycoproteins.
    • α-subunit is shared with TSH and hCG — this is why hCG can cross-react at LH receptors (trigger shots in IVF; gestational thyrotoxicosis at TSHR).FA 2024 Endo
  • Pulse frequency bias:
    • Slow GnRH pulses → FSH-dominant release.
    • Fast GnRH pulses → LH-dominant release (part of why PCOS has LH > FSH).
  • Continuous GnRH = shutdown:
    • Gonadotropes down-regulate GnRH receptors when signal is continuous.
    • Leuprolide depot exploits this (prostate CA, precocious puberty, endometriosis). Initial \"flare\" before suppression.

2 · Two-cell, two-gonadotropin model — the test favorite

Division of labor
  • Theca cells:
    • Have LH receptors.
    • Have cholesterol desmolase + 17α-hydroxylase + 17,20-lyase → can make androgens.
    • Do NOT express aromatase → cannot finish estradiol synthesis alone.
  • Granulosa cells:
    • Have FSH receptors.
    • Express aromatase (CYP19A1).
    • Lack 17α-hydroxylase/17,20-lyase → depend on theca-derived androgens as aromatase substrate.
  • Cell-cell handoff:
    • Androstenedione / T diffuse across the basement membrane from theca to granulosa.
    • Granulosa aromatizes them to estrogen.
Late-follicular change
  • Granulosa gain LH receptors:
    • Only the dominant follicle upregulates LH-R on granulosa late in the follicular phase.
    • Enables the LH surge to trigger ovulation and local progesterone secretion.

3 · Negative vs positive feedback — the concentration/duration rule

Negative feedback (most of the cycle)
  • Moderate E2:
    • Inhibits GnRH at the arcuate nucleus and the gonadotropes.
    • Combined OCPs exploit this by holding E2 steady below the surge threshold.
  • Progesterone (luteal):
    • Powerful negative feedback; slows pulse generator → slow, low-amplitude LH pulses.
    • Blocks positive feedback at AVPV even if E2 is high → no second surge in luteal phase.
  • Inhibin B / A:
    • Selectively suppress FSH at the pituitary.
    • Inhibin B = early follicular; Inhibin A = luteal.
Positive feedback (once per cycle)
  • Threshold:
    • E2 must be > ~200 pg/mL for ≥ 48 h — intensity AND duration matter.Guyton 14e ch 75
  • Mechanism:
    • AVPV kisspeptin neurons sense high E2 → burst of GnRH → pituitary LH spike (10× baseline).
    • Smaller FSH surge, limited by inhibin B.
    • Ovulation ~36 h after the surge begins.

4 · Kisspeptin — the master activator, two populations

The two kisspeptin populations
  • AVPV kisspeptin:
    • Anteroventral periventricular nucleus (within POA).
    • Stimulated by sustained high E2 → positive feedback → LH surge.
    • Apoptoses in males perinatally from aromatized androgen → why males cannot mount an LH surge.
  • Arcuate kisspeptin:
    • Inhibited by E2 → negative feedback and tonic pulse generation.
    • Preserved in males.
Clinical relevance of kisspeptin
  • Disorders:
    • KISS1R loss-of-function → hypogonadotropic hypogonadism (no puberty).
    • KISS1R gain-of-function → central precocious puberty.
    • Kisspeptin analogs: under study as alternative ovulation triggers in IVF.

5 · Stress, metabolic, and neural inputs to GnRH

Modulators of the GnRH neuron
  • Activators:
    • Kisspeptin (primary activator).
    • Norepinephrine.
  • Inhibitors:
    • β-endorphins (runner's amenorrhea).
    • GnIH (gonadotropin-inhibiting hormone) — induced by glucocorticoids.
    • Dopamine (indirect via prolactin when dopamine is low).
  • Permissive factors:
    • Leptin — signals energy availability to kisspeptin neurons. Low leptin → low kisspeptin → low GnRH → hypothalamic amenorrhea.
  • Glucocorticoids (stress):
    • Inhibit every tier: ↓ GnRH, ↑ GnIH, ↓ LH/FSH release, ↓ ovarian steroidogenesis.
    • Clinical: functional hypothalamic amenorrhea, ICU illness, female athlete triad.
  • Prolactin:
    • Inhibits GnRH → hyperprolactinemia → amenorrhea-galactorrhea. Prolactinoma → treat with dopamine agonist (cabergoline).

4Ovarian follicle & corpus luteum development

The Gist A female is born with her entire lifetime supply of oocytes, all arrested in prophase I of meiosis. Each month a cohort enters the stage parade — primordial → primary → secondary → antral → Graafian — but only one dominant follicle ovulates. The rest undergo atresia. Each stage has its own endocrine signature: primordial cells are dormant, antral follicles make escalating estradiol + inhibin B, and the post-ovulatory corpus luteum becomes the body's progesterone factory. If the CL isn't rescued by trophoblastic hCG, it involutes into a corpus albicans and menses follows.

1 · The primordial pool & meiotic arrest

Where oocytes are arrested
  • Prophase I arrest (in utero until ovulation):
    • All primordial oocytes pause in the diplotene stage of prophase I from fetal life.FA 2024 Repro
    • LH surge resumes meiosis I → secondary oocyte + first polar body.
  • Metaphase II arrest (until fertilization):
    • After ovulation, the secondary oocyte arrests at metaphase II.
    • Fertilization triggers completion → 2nd polar body + haploid egg.
Clinical significance
  • Aging egg pool:
    • Longer MI arrest → ↑ risk of non-disjunction → aneuploidies with advanced maternal age (trisomy 21, 18, 13).
    • Declining AMH reflects shrinking antral pool → counseled in fertility care.

2 · Follicle stages — who expresses what receptor

Receptor expression timeline
  • Theca cells:
    • Express LH receptors from the secondary stage onward.
    • Keep making androgens for granulosa throughout antral/Graafian stages.
  • Granulosa cells:
    • Express FSH receptors throughout.
    • LH receptors appear late (Graafian stage).
    • This late LH-R expression is what enables the surge to trigger ovulation + luteinization.Costanzo 6e ch 10; B&B Repro
Hormone contributions per stage
  • Primordial + primary:
    • Essentially no measurable endocrine output.
    • Their development is partly FSH-independent (gonadotropin-independent phase).
  • Secondary → antral:
    • Now gonadotropin-dependent.
    • Theca androgens + granulosa aromatase → escalating estradiol.
    • Granulosa also makes inhibin B.
  • Graafian:
    • Peak estradiol, peak inhibin B.
    • Late in the stage, granulosa begins making some progesterone locally (primes for ovulation).

3 · Dominant follicle selection — winner take all

The FSH-threshold model
  • FSH rises after CL regression (loss of inhibin A).
  • A cohort of ~15–20 antral follicles begins to grow together in response to rising FSH.
  • The most FSH-sensitive follicle grows fastest → the \"dominant\" follicle.
  • Its own escalating E2 + inhibin B suppress FSH → weaker follicles undergo atresia.Guyton 14e ch 82
Why clinical therapies break this loop
  • Clomiphene / letrozole:
    • Clomiphene (SERM) blocks hypothalamic ER → removes negative feedback → FSH stays high → multiple follicles escape atresia.
    • Letrozole (aromatase inhibitor) lowers E2 production → same FSH rise.
    • Explains increased dizygotic twins with ovulation induction.
  • PCOS:
    • Relatively low FSH + excess theca androgens → multiple follicles arrested at antral stage → \"string of pearls\" on ultrasound.
    • No dominant follicle selected → chronic anovulation.
AMH as a marker of reserve
  • Small antral follicles make AMH:
    • Serum AMH correlates with the size of the antral pool.
    • Low AMH: diminished ovarian reserve, primary ovarian insufficiency, menopause approaching.
    • High AMH: PCOS (lots of arrested small antral follicles).

4 · Corpus luteum → corpus albicans

Formation
  • LH surge triggers luteinization:
    • Granulosa & theca cells hypertrophy, acquire lipid droplets, look yellow (→ \"luteum\").
    • Granulosa-derived cells become \"large luteal cells\"; theca-derived become \"small luteal cells\".
  • Vascularization:
    • Blood vessels invade the previously avascular follicle → CL can reach higher hormone output than the Graafian follicle.
  • Corpus hemorrhagicum → CL:
    • Immediately after ovulation the follicle bleeds (corpus hemorrhagicum) before becoming yellow CL.
    • Pelvic ultrasound in early luteal phase may show a hemorrhagic cyst — usually physiologic.
Endocrine output
  • Progesterone (dominant):
    • Drives secretory endometrium, thickens cervical mucus, inhibits myometrium, raises basal body temperature.
  • Estradiol:
    • Maintains endometrium; reinforces negative feedback (blocks another surge).
  • Inhibin A:
    • Selectively suppresses FSH → prevents new follicle recruitment during the luteal phase.
  • Relaxin (late pregnancy):
    • Softens cervix, loosens ligaments — contributes to pregnancy accommodation.
Fate
  • No pregnancy:
    • CL lifespan is ~12 days without hCG rescue.
    • Involution → fibrous scar = corpus albicans.
    • Progesterone & estradiol fall → endometrium sloughs → menses.
  • Pregnancy:
    • Trophoblast hCG cross-reacts with LH-R on CL → rescues it.
    • CL makes progesterone until ~8–10 weeks; placenta takes over (luteoplacental shift).
    • After the shift, oophorectomy is tolerated without miscarriage.
★ HIGH-YIELD for USMLE

  • CL lifespan = 12 days without hCG rescue. CL makes progesterone until the luteoplacental shift at 8–10 weeks.

  • AMH tracks the antral pool: low in POI/menopause; high in PCOS.
  • String of pearls + hirsutism + anovulation = PCOS (failure of dominant follicle selection).

Early follicular (days 1–7)
  • FSH rises:

2 · LH surge & ovulation — mechanics in detail

Triggering the surge
  • AVPV kisspeptin:
    • Sustained high E2 activates AVPV → burst GnRH release → LH spike (~10× baseline).
    • Smaller FSH surge (limited by inhibin B).
  • Timing:
    • Surge lasts ~24–48 h.
    • Ovulation occurs ~36 h after LH begins rising, ~10–12 h after LH peak.FA 2024 Repro
Mechanism of follicle rupture
  • Granulosa cells (LH-R positive):
    • Make local progesterone that activates proteolytic enzymes (collagenase, plasminogen activators).
    • Follicle wall weakens; antral fluid pressure rises.
  • Stigma → rupture:
    • A thin-walled bulge (stigma) forms on the follicle surface, ruptures, and the ovum + cumulus are released into the fimbriated tube.
Meiosis timing at ovulation
  • Before surge: primary oocyte arrested prophase I.
  • LH surge → completes meiosis I → secondary oocyte + 1st polar body.
  • Post-ovulation: arrests at metaphase II until fertilization.
  • Fertilization completes meiosis II → 2nd polar body.

3 · Luteal phase — the \"waiting for implantation\" phase

Hormone pattern
  • Progesterone rises sharply:
    • Peaks ~day 21–22 (mid-luteal).
    • Day-21 serum P > 5–10 ng/mL = ovulatory cycle confirmation.
  • Estradiol stays elevated (CL source).
  • Inhibin A rises → selectively suppresses FSH → prevents new follicle recruitment.
Uterine response (secretory phase)
  • Glands become tortuous & sacculated; glycogen-rich; subnuclear vacuoles appear ~day 16–18 (\"early secretory\").
  • Spiral arteries coil.
  • Stroma decidualizes in preparation for implantation.
  • Cervical mucus thickens (blocks sperm + microbes).
  • Myometrium quiescent (progesterone effect).
If no pregnancy
  • CL lacks sustained LH support → involutes after ~12 days.
  • P & E2 fall → spiral arteries constrict → endometrial ischemia → menses.
  • Fall in inhibin A removes FSH brake → FSH rises → next cycle starts.

4 · Pregnancy & the luteoplacental shift

Early pregnancy
  • Trophoblast secretes β-hCG:
    • Detectable in serum ~8–10 days post-ovulation.
    • Doubles every 48 h in normal early pregnancy.
    • Binds LH-R on CL → CL keeps making progesterone.
  • hCG peaks ~10 weeks, then declines.
Luteoplacental shift (~8–10 weeks)
  • Placenta takes over:
    • Placental syncytiotrophoblast makes sufficient progesterone to maintain pregnancy.
    • Oophorectomy after this point is tolerated without miscarriage.
Maternal & fetal hormone changes
  • SHBG rises:
    • Driven by placental estrogens; buffers steroid bioavailability.
  • Fetal-placental estriol:
    • Fetal adrenal DHEA-S → placenta → estriol. Maternal serum estriol used in prenatal screening.

3 · PCOS — the metabolic anovulation syndrome

Endocrine loop
  • Fast GnRH pulses → LH:FSH > 2–3:1:
    • High LH stimulates theca → excess androgens (androstenedione, T).
    • Relatively low FSH → granulosa aromatase underactive → follicles arrested at antral stage.
  • Peripheral aromatization:
    • Adipose tissue converts androstenedione → estrone (E1).
    • Chronic low-level estrone without progesterone = unopposed estrogen → endometrial hyperplasia → cancer risk.
  • Insulin resistance:
    • Hyperinsulinemia stimulates theca androgen production and lowers SHBG (more free T).

  • Oligo/anovulation (oligomenorrhea or amenorrhea).
  • Clinical or biochemical hyperandrogenism (hirsutism, acne, ↑ free T).
  • Polycystic ovaries on ultrasound (≥ 12 small antral follicles).
  • Associated: obesity, insulin resistance/T2DM, acanthosis nigricans, metabolic syndrome, OSA.
Treatment
  • Lifestyle: weight loss can restore ovulation.
  • Combined OCPs: cycle regulation + reduce free T (↑ SHBG) + protect endometrium.
  • Metformin: addresses insulin resistance.
  • Clomiphene / letrozole: ovulation induction (letrozole now first-line).
  • Spironolactone: AR antagonist for hirsutism (teratogenic — use contraception).
  • Cyclic progestin or OCP protects endometrium.

4 · Menopause & HRT

Endocrine changes
  • Sequence:
    • Declining follicle pool → ↓ inhibin B first (earliest perimenopausal change).
    • FSH rises next (inhibin loss).
    • Eventually ovary can't make estradiol → ↑↑ FSH/LH, ↓↓ E2.
    • FSH rises more than LH because inhibin is also gone.
  • Remaining estrogen source:
    • Peripheral aromatization of adrenal androgens to estrone (E1) in adipose tissue.
Symptoms (\"HAVOCS\")
  • H — Hot flashes / vasomotor symptoms (LH pulses → cutaneous vasodilation).
  • A — Atrophy of vagina → dyspareunia, recurrent UTIs.
  • V — Vaginal dryness (genitourinary syndrome of menopause).
  • O — Osteoporosis (estrogen inhibits osteoclasts).
  • C — Coronary artery disease risk rises.
  • S — Sleep disturbance, mood changes, cognitive fuzziness.
Hormone replacement therapy
  • Benefits:
    • Symptom relief (vasomotor, GSM), bone density preservation.
  • Risks:
    • VTE / stroke (estrogen prothrombotic).
    • Breast cancer (long-term combined HRT).
    • Endometrial cancer if estrogen is unopposed in a patient with a uterus.
  • Rules:
    • Uterus intact → estrogen + progestin.
    • Post-hysterectomy → estrogen alone.
    • Timing hypothesis: risk-benefit most favorable when started < 60 or within 10 years of menopause.
  • Alternatives for vasomotor symptoms: SSRIs/SNRIs, gabapentin, clonidine, lifestyle.

5 · Stress, EDCs, and intersex summary

Stress-induced reproductive dysfunction
  • Glucocorticoids inhibit every level of the HPG axis (GnRH, LH/FSH, gonadal steroidogenesis) and facilitate GnIH.
  • Clinical: FHA, reduced libido, impaired spermatogenesis under chronic stress.
  • Severe acute illness (ICU) → transient HPG suppression that typically recovers.
Endocrine-disrupting compounds (EDCs)
  • Examples: BPA, phthalates, DES, PCBs, certain pesticides.
  • Population trends associated with exposure: earlier puberty, altered sperm counts, hypospadias.
  • Developmental windows matter — organizational effects can be lifelong.
Intersex / DSD spectrum
  • 46,XX DSD: CAH is the most common cause of ambiguous newborn genitalia.
  • 46,XY DSD: AIS, 5α-RD, Swyer, persistent Müllerian duct syndrome.
  • Sex chromosome DSD: Turner, Klinefelter, mosaics.
  • Multidisciplinary care; long-term concerns include bone health, cardiovascular risk, and cancer risk in retained Y-gonads.
★ HIGH-YIELD for USMLE
  • Hi-Lo = primary hypogonadism (brain is yelling at a broken gonad).
  • Lo-Lo = secondary hypogonadism (brain quiet, gonad quiet).
  • Hi-Hi / High-T ineffective = receptor defect (AIS).
  • PCOS: LH:FSH > 2, insulin resistance, unopposed estrogen → endometrial cancer risk.
  • Kallmann: anosmia + hypogonadotropic hypogonadism.
  • POI: menopausal labs < 40 — karyotype + fragile X + autoimmune workup.
  • Menopause: FSH rises more than LH (inhibin loss); estrone is the main residual estrogen.
  • HRT: uterus intact → E + P. Unopposed estrogen → endometrial carcinoma.
🧠 MEMORY TRICK

\"Hi-Lo, Lo-Lo, Hi-Hi.\" Primary = high gonadotropins, low steroids. Secondary = low both. Receptor defect = high steroids that don't work.

PCOS = HAIR: Hyperandrogenism, Anovulation, Insulin resistance, Rotterdam + \"string of pearls\".

HAVOCS = menopause symptoms: Hot flashes, Atrophy, Vaginal dryness, Osteoporosis, CAD, Sleep/mood.

Kallmann clue: \"can't smell, can't swell\" — anosmia + failure of puberty.

🏥 CLINICAL PEARL
  • Turner syndrome — echocardiogram for bicuspid aortic valve / coarctation is mandatory.
  • Prolactinoma — first-line treatment is a dopamine agonist (cabergoline), not surgery.
  • Female athlete triad — stress fractures at unusual sites should prompt workup.
  • PCOS + postmenopausal bleeding — endometrial biopsy to rule out hyperplasia/carcinoma.
  • HRT in young POI patient — different risk-benefit than HRT in standard menopause; higher doses to protect bone & cardiovascular system.

A 34-year-old has FSH 68, LH 42, E2 12, hot flashes, 6 months amenorrhea. Diagnosis?
Primary ovarian insufficiency (POI). Work up karyotype, fragile X premutation, autoimmune screen. HRT until age ~51.
Which single symptom distinguishes Kallmann from other causes of hypogonadotropic hypogonadism?
Anosmia (or hyposmia) — GnRH and olfactory neurons share embryologic migration from the olfactory placode.
Why do PCOS patients have unopposed estrogen despite androgen excess?
Peripheral aromatization converts excess androstenedione to estrone. With chronic anovulation there is no corpus luteum and no progesterone to oppose it.
Why does FSH rise more than LH in menopause?
Loss of ovarian inhibin (which selectively suppresses FSH) adds to the loss of estradiol negative feedback. Both gonadotropins rise, FSH more.
First-line treatment for prolactinoma causing amenorrhea-galactorrhea?
Dopamine agonist — cabergoline (or bromocriptine). Surgery reserved for dopamine-agonist resistance or mass effect.
Why must a woman on HRT with an intact uterus receive a progestin?
Unopposed estrogen → endometrial hyperplasia → adenocarcinoma. Progestin protects the endometrium. Post-hysterectomy patients can use estrogen alone.
Newborn girl with ambiguous genitalia, hyponatremia, hyperkalemia, hypotension, elevated 17-OH-progesterone. Diagnosis and enzyme?
21-hydroxylase deficiency CAH. Deficiency of cortisol + aldosterone → salt-wasting crisis; shunted substrate → androgen excess → in-utero virilization of a 46,XX fetus.
