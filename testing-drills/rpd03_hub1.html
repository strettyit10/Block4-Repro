Ovary Organization
Name the three basic regions of the adult ovary from outermost to innermost, and what each contains.
tap to flip
Answer
1. Germinal epithelium — simple cuboidal surface; underlying tunica albuginea (dense CT).

2. Cortex — connective tissue containing ovarian follicles in varying stages.

3. Medulla — loose CT with blood vessels, nerves, lymphatics.

Clinical: Most ovarian cancers arise from the germinal epithelium.
Primordial Follicle
What cells surround the oocyte in a primordial follicle, and at what stage of meiosis is the oocyte arrested?
tap to flip
Answer
Primordial follicle: oocyte surrounded by a single layer of squamous follicular cells (+ stromal cells outside).

All follicles exist in this stage at birth, arrested in prophase I of meiosis.

Do NOT respond to FSH until puberty. Each cycle, ~20 are recruited and upgrade their follicular cells to columnar/granular (granulosa cells).
Primary Follicles
Differentiate unilaminar vs. multilaminar primary follicles. When does the zona pellucida form?
tap to flip
Answer
Unilaminar primary follicle: oocyte surrounded by a single layer of granulosa cells (cuboidal/columnar) + basal lamina. Zona pellucida begins forming.

Multilaminar primary follicle: hyperplasia → multiple layers of granulosa cells. Now the zona pellucida is fully visible (proteoglycan-rich ECM between oocyte & granulosa — synthesized by BOTH). Stromal cells begin differentiating into theca interna.
Secondary (Antral) Follicle
What defines the secondary follicle? What is OMI, and what does the theca interna do that the theca externa doesn't?
tap to flip
Answer
Defined by the antrum — a fluid-filled space containing hyaluronic acid-rich liquor folliculi with OMI (Oocyte Meiosis Inhibitor), which arrests the oocyte in prophase II.

Theca interna: steroidogenic cells → produce androstenedione → aromatized by granulosa cells into estrogen (2-cell hypothesis).

Theca externa: mainly connective tissue — structural only.
Graafian (Tertiary) Follicle
What are the corona radiata and cumulus oophorus, and where is the oocyte located in a Graafian follicle?
tap to flip
Answer
Graafian follicle = dominant follicle. Oocyte is eccentrically located, surrounded by granulosa cells connected to the mature follicle by a granulosa pedicle.

Corona radiata: innermost single layer of granulosa cells directly hugging the oocyte (travels WITH the oocyte at ovulation).

Cumulus oophorus: accumulations of granulosa cells continuous with the pedicle — attaches the oocyte complex to the follicle wall.
Ovulation — LH Surge
What triggers the LH surge, and what happens to meiosis at ovulation?
tap to flip
Answer
Rising estrogen (from the dominant Graafian follicle) → LH surge.

Follicle ruptures → oocyte releases with corona radiata attached = ovulation.

LH overrides OMI → meiosis resumes, proceeds through meiosis I, then arrests again in metaphase II — where it stays until fertilization.
Corpus Luteum
After ovulation, what do granulosa and theca interna cells become, and what do they each now secrete?
tap to flip
Answer
Remnants of the ruptured Graafian follicle → corpus luteum ("yellow body"), a temporary endocrine gland.

Granulosa cells (estrogen-producing) → granulosa lutein cells → now produce progesterone.

Theca interna cells (androstenedione-secreting) → theca lutein cells → now produce estrogen.

If no pregnancy: CL regresses → corpus albicans (white scar).
Oviduct — 4 Parts
Name the four parts of the fallopian tube from ovary to uterus and their key functions.
tap to flip
Answer
1. Infundibulum — initial trumpet-shaped segment; fimbriae move to trap the ovum.

2. Ampulla — most dilated, complex mucosal folding; site of fertilization.

3. Isthmus — shortest; connects to uterus.

4. Intramural (pars interstitialis) — passes through uterine wall.

Mnemonic: IAII = "I Ain't Implanting Inside."
Oviduct Epithelium
What two cell types line the fallopian tube, and what hormone maintains their function?
tap to flip
Answer
Simple columnar epithelium with two cell types — both estrogen-dependent:

Ciliated cells: help move the ovum/zygote through the tube.

Non-ciliated peg (secretory) cells: secrete proteins to nurture the zygote.

Wall layers: mucosa → muscularis (inner circular + outer longitudinal) → serosa.
Uterus — 3 Wall Layers
Name the three layers of the uterine wall and the two sublayers of the endometrium. Which sublayer sheds during menses?
tap to flip
Answer
1. Endometrium — innermost; simple columnar (ciliated + secretory) + simple tubular glands.
• Functionalis: superficial layer sheds at menstruation (supplied by spiral/coiled arteries).
• Basalis: deep layer, provides cells to regenerate the functionalis (supplied by straight arteries).

2. Myometrium — thickest middle smooth muscle; hypertrophy & hyperplasia during pregnancy.

3. Perimetrium — outer CT; forms part of broad ligament.
HPO Axis
Trace the hypothalamic-pituitary-ovarian axis: hormones, targets, and feedback loops.
tap to flip
Answer
Hypothalamus: GnRH → ant. pituitary
Ant. pituitary:
• FSH → stimulates follicular phase
• LH → stimulates ovulation + corpus luteum

Follicles → estrogen → negative feedback on hypothalamus (GnRH) UNTIL a threshold → positive feedback → LH surge.

Corpus luteum → estrogen + progesterone → negative feedback on hypothalamus + pituitary.
Menstrual Cycle Phases
Name and time the four phases of the menstrual cycle and the dominant hormone of each.
tap to flip
Answer
1. Menses (day 0–5): ↓estrogen, ↓progesterone (CL has degenerated).
2. Proliferative (day 6–14) / Follicular: estrogen rises → endometrium thickens (2–3×), straight tubular glands elongate.
3. Secretory (day 15–28) / Luteal: CL produces estrogen + progesterone → endometrium thickens more, glands become coiled & convoluted, spiral arteries elongate; glands secrete for implantation.
4. Ischemic (late day 28): CL degenerates → ↓E, ↓P → vasospasm of spiral arteries → ischemic necrosis of functionalis → menses restart.
Cervix — Epithelium & T-Zone
What type of epithelium lines the ectocervix vs. endocervix, and where does cervical cancer most often arise?
tap to flip
Answer
Ectocervix (projects into vagina): stratified squamous non-keratinized.

Endocervix (canal): simple columnar glandular (secretes mucus).

Transformation zone (T-zone): the junction between squamous and columnar — where HPV (16, 18) → most cervical cancers arise. Gardasil prevents.
Vagina — Histology
What are the three layers of the vaginal wall and the type of epithelium in the mucosa?
tap to flip
Answer
1. Mucosa: stratified squamous non-keratinized epithelium (like ectocervix); no glands (lubricated by cervical mucus + Bartholin glands).

2. Muscularis: smooth muscle — inner circular, outer longitudinal.

3. Serosa/Adventitia: dense CT + elastic fibers.

Key: estrogen stimulates glycogen deposition → fermented by Lactobacillus → acidic pH.
External Genitalia & Glands
Describe the histology of the clitoris, labia minora, and labia majora. Name the homologues of Bartholin's and Skene glands.
tap to flip
Answer
Clitoris: homologue of the penis — venous erectile tissue (corpora cavernosa), blood vessels, sensory nerves (Pacinian corpuscles).
Labia minora: core spongy tissue + keratinized stratified squamous epithelium.
Labia majora: skin + adipose + thin smooth muscle; hair, sebaceous, sweat glands; keratinized stratified squamous.

Bartholin's glands (greater vestibular) — simple columnar; mucous lubricant → homologue: bulbourethral (Cowper) glands.
Skene glands (paraurethral) — pseudostratified/simple columnar; lubricant → homologue: prostate.
Mammary Glands
What is the basic structural unit of the mammary gland? What changes in active (lactating) vs. inactive glands?
tap to flip
Answer
Compound tubuloalveolar glands grouped into lobes separated by dense CT (and fat). Milk drains through branching ducts → lactiferous ducts → lactiferous sinuses → nipple.

Inactive gland: predominantly ducts + adipose/CT, few secretory units.
Active (lactating) gland: extensive tubuloalveolar secretory units with lumens full of milk; reduced stromal fat.

Changes driven by: estrogen (ducts), progesterone (alveoli), prolactin (milk production), oxytocin (let-down reflex).

Test Trap Mode: Each statement is either TRUE or FALSE as written. Many are designed to catch common exam errors. Click T or F — then read the explanation carefully.
Trap 01 — Oocyte Arrest
"At birth, all primordial follicles contain oocytes arrested in metaphase II of meiosis."
FALSE. Primordial follicles are arrested in prophase I at birth — where they stay until puberty. After the LH surge at ovulation, meiosis resumes and re-arrests in metaphase II. Metaphase II arrest only occurs AFTER ovulation and is only broken by fertilization. Classic order: prophase I → prophase II (in antral follicles, via OMI) → metaphase II.
Trap 02 — 2-Cell Hypothesis
"Granulosa cells produce androgens, which theca interna cells then convert into estrogen."
FALSE. Completely reversed. The theca interna cells (LH-stimulated) produce androstenedione, which the granulosa cells (FSH-stimulated, expressing aromatase) convert into estrogen. Mnemonic: "LH likes L-theca; FSH feeds F-granulosa." This is the classic 2-cell 2-gonadotropin model.
Trap 03 — Corpus Luteum
"In the corpus luteum, granulosa lutein cells still produce estrogen while theca lutein cells now produce progesterone."
FALSE. Also reversed. In the CL, granulosa lutein cells NOW make progesterone (their main CL product) — a switch from the estrogen they made before ovulation. Theca lutein cells now make estrogen — also a switch from androstenedione. This hormone "swap" is why the corpus luteum secretes both progesterone (dominant) + estrogen in the secretory phase.
Trap 04 — Endometrial Regeneration
"After menstruation, the functionalis regenerates the basalis."
FALSE. Backwards. The basalis (deep layer) is preserved during menses and regenerates the functionalis (superficial layer) in the proliferative phase. The functionalis is what sheds. Basalis is supplied by straight arteries (not hormonally sensitive), while functionalis is supplied by spiral/coiled arteries (estrogen/progesterone-responsive; constrict during ischemic phase).
Trap 05 — Ectocervix Epithelium
"The ectocervix is lined by stratified squamous KERATINIZED epithelium, like the skin."
FALSE. The ectocervix (and vagina) are lined by stratified squamous NON-keratinized epithelium — because they are moist internal surfaces. Keratinization is reserved for dry external surfaces (skin, labia minora/majora surface). The non-keratinized ectocervix meets the simple columnar endocervix at the transformation zone, the primary site of HPV-driven cervical cancer.
Trap 06 — Fertilization Site
"Fertilization most commonly occurs in the isthmus of the fallopian tube because it is the shortest segment."
FALSE. Fertilization occurs in the ampulla, not the isthmus. The ampulla is the longest and most dilated segment with complex mucosal folds that create a favorable environment (transudative fluid accumulation). Sperm travel uterus → isthmus → ampulla, where they encounter the ovum. The isthmus is the shortest segment closer to the uterus. Ectopic pregnancy most commonly occurs in the ampulla for the same reason.
Trap 07 — Menstrual Cycle Timing
"The luteal (secretory) phase varies considerably in length, while the follicular (proliferative) phase is fixed at ~14 days."
FALSE. Reversed. The luteal phase is FIXED at ~14 days (determined by corpus luteum lifespan — CL degenerates after ~14 days if no hCG). The follicular phase is VARIABLE — this is why cycle length differs between women. Clinical pearl: ovulation is ~14 days BEFORE the next menstrual period (not 14 days after the current one).
Trap 08 — Corpus Luteum-Placental Shift
"If pregnancy occurs, the corpus luteum must produce progesterone for the entire 9-month gestation."
FALSE. The corpus luteum-placental shift: hCG from the implanting blastocyst maintains the CL through the first trimester. Around weeks 8–10, the placenta takes over production of progesterone and estrogen — and the CL gradually regresses. Clinical relevance: removing the CL (e.g., oophorectomy) before week 10 risks miscarriage; after that, the placenta alone sustains pregnancy.
