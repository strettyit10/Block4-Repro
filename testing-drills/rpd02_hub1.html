Germ Layers
From which germ layer does the majority of the urogenital system derive? Name three exceptions.
tap to flip
Answer
Intermediate mesoderm gives rise to most of the urogenital tract.

Exceptions:
• Adrenal medulla → ectoderm (neural crest)
• Genital swellings → ectoderm
• Urogenital sinus → endoderm
Pseudo-Kidneys
Name the three kidney precursors in chronological order, their timing, and which one makes urine.
tap to flip
Answer
Pronephros — start of week 4 (neck region); does NOT form urine; regresses by end of wk 4.

Mesonephros — end of wk 4 through wk 10; forms urine (glomerulus-like filtration); drains into cloaca.

Metanephros — week 5; becomes the definitive kidney; starts making urine at week 10.
Reciprocal Induction
What two structures signal each other to form the definitive kidney, and what does each contribute?
tap to flip
Answer
Ureteric bud (from mesonephric duct) ↔ metanephric blastema (from intermediate mesoderm) — each induces the other to grow.

Metanephros → NEPHRON: Bowman's capsule, PCT, LOH, DCT, cortex & medulla.
Ureteric bud → COLLECTING system: collecting duct, minor/major calyces, renal pelvis, ureter.
Kidney Ascent
When do the kidneys ascend, what blocks them from ascending if fused, and what's the final adult arterial supply?
tap to flip
Answer
Kidneys ascend just before nephrons develop (after ureteric bud bifurcates).

If fused (horseshoe kidney), ascent is blocked by the IMA (inferior mesenteric artery).

Final supply: abdominal aorta (renal arteries). Persistent accessory vessels → anatomical variation.
Cloaca Splitting
What divides the cloaca? What two cavities result, and what does each become?
tap to flip
Answer
The urorectal septum divides the cloaca starting at week 4 (~day 30).

Anterior → urogenital sinus → primitive bladder, urethra, lower vagina/prostatic urethra.
Posterior → anorectal canal → rectum & anal canal.
Trigone Origin
What forms the smooth trigone of the bladder, and why is this embryological origin different from the rest of the bladder?
tap to flip
Answer
The mesonephric (Wolffian) ducts get absorbed into the developing bladder during weeks 5–6, forming the vesical trigone — a triangular smooth region containing the openings of the ureters and urethra.

The rest of the bladder is derived from endoderm (urogenital sinus), so the trigone is mesodermal in origin while the bladder dome is endodermal.
SRY Gene
What does SRY encode, and which cells secrete MIS vs. testosterone in the developing testis?
tap to flip
Answer
SRY (on Y chromosome) codes for TDF (Testis Determining Factor).

Sertoli cells (from primitive sex cords) → MIS/AMH → regresses Müllerian ducts.

Leydig cells (weeks 8–10) → testosterone → maintains Wolffian ducts.

Testosterone → DHT via 5α-reductase → masculinizes external genitalia.
Duct Fates
What do the mesonephric (Wolffian) and paramesonephric (Müllerian) ducts become in males vs. females?
tap to flip
Answer
Wolffian (mesonephric) duct:
• ♂ → "SEED": Seminal vesicles · Epididymis · Ejaculatory duct · Ductus (vas) deferens (+ trigone)
• ♀ → degenerates (remnants = Gartner's duct cysts)

Müllerian (paramesonephric) duct:
• ♂ → degenerates (MIS from Sertoli)
• ♀ → fallopian tubes, uterus, cervix, upper 2/3 vagina
DHT — External Genitalia
What does DHT do to external genital primordia in males? What happens in females without DHT?
tap to flip
Answer
DHT present (♂):
• Genital tubercle → glans/shaft of penis
• Urogenital (urethral) folds → fuse ventrally → penile urethra
• Labioscrotal swellings → fuse midline → scrotum (with raphe)

DHT absent (♀ default):
• Tubercle → clitoris
• Urethral folds → labia minora (unfused)
• Labioscrotal swellings → labia majora (unfused)
Potter Sequence
What is the pneumonic "POTTER," what causes it, and why is it usually fatal?
tap to flip
Answer
POTTER: Pulmonary hypoplasia · Oligohydramnios · Twisted face · Twisted skin · Extremity defects · Renal failure

Causes: bilateral renal agenesis, polycystic kidney disease, obstructive uropathy, VACTERL defects (esophageal atresia → can't swallow amniotic fluid).

Lethal from pulmonary hypoplasia — lungs can't mature without amniotic fluid (fetal urine makes amniotic fluid in T2).
Horseshoe Kidney
What is a horseshoe kidney, where does it get stuck, and what is the classic imaging association?
tap to flip
Answer
Lower poles of both kidneys fuse in the midline (both metanephric blastemas meet near midline). The U-shaped organ tries to ascend but gets trapped under the IMA.

Usually asymptomatic; found incidentally on prenatal US or CT/MRI/IVP. ↑ association with Turner syndrome and ↑ risk of UTI, nephrolithiasis, renal cancer.
MCDK
What causes Multicystic Dysplastic Kidney (MCDK), how is it different from ADPKD, and what's its prognosis?
tap to flip
Answer
MCDK: abnormal interaction between ureteric bud & metanephric blastema during initial kidney development → non-functioning cysts replace normal tissue.

Unilateral: usually asymptomatic — contralateral kidney compensates.
Bilateral: incompatible with life (Potter sequence).

Differs from ADPKD: MCDK is a developmental sporadic defect; ADPKD is genetic (PKD1/PKD2), presents in adulthood with hypertension and renal failure.
Hypospadias vs Epispadias
What fails in hypospadias? In epispadias? Which is more common and which is associated with bladder exstrophy?
tap to flip
Answer
Hypospadias (more common) — urethral folds fail to fuse → abnormal urethral opening on the ventral (inferior) shaft of penis. Most defects are distal (glanular, subcoronal).

Epispadias — faulty positioning of genital tubercle → opening on the dorsal (superior) side. Associated with bladder exstrophy.

Treat with urethroplasty (buccal mucosa graft) around 6–12 months.
Cryptorchidism
What is cryptorchidism, when is orchidopexy performed, and why does timing matter?
tap to flip
Answer
Failure of testicular descent through the inguinal canal into the scrotum. Guided by the gubernaculum.

If not descended by 6 months → orchidopexy.

Early surgery lowers risk of:
• Seminoma (testicular malignancy)
• Infertility (heat-sensitive spermatogenesis)
• Inguinal hernia

Also → ↓risk of torsion & improves cosmesis.
21-Hydroxylase Deficiency (CAH)
Why does 21-hydroxylase deficiency virilize XX females? What electrolytes/BP findings are classic?
tap to flip
Answer
95% of CAH cases. 21-OH is needed for aldosterone & cortisol — when blocked, precursors shunt to androgens (testosterone, DHT).

In XX: excess androgens → virilized/ambiguous genitalia.

Labs: ↓aldosterone → salt wasting, hyponatremia, hyperkalemia, hypotension; ↑17-OH-progesterone (screening marker); ↓cortisol → hypoglycemia.

Contrast: 11β-OH def → HTN (desoxycortico­sterone buildup). 17α-OH def → HTN + no virilization (XX appears phenotypically normal, XY looks female).
5α-Reductase Deficiency vs AIS
How do 5α-reductase deficiency and complete androgen insensitivity differ at puberty and in hormone levels?
tap to flip
Answer
5α-reductase deficiency (46 XY):
• Testosterone normal, ↑T:DHT ratio
• Female/ambiguous external genitalia at birth
• At puberty, ↑testosterone → virilization ("penis at 12")
• Internal male structures present (Wolffian derivatives)

Complete AIS (46 XY):
• Defective androgen receptor
• Normal/↑ testosterone + ↑ LH
• Female external genitalia, blind vagina
• No Müllerian derivatives (MIS still produced)
• At puberty → breast development (peripheral aromatization); amenorrhea
• Gonadectomy AFTER puberty (↑malignancy risk)

Test Trap Mode: Each statement is either TRUE or FALSE as written. Many are designed to catch common exam errors. Click T or F — then read the explanation carefully.
Trap 01 — Germ Layers
"The entire adrenal gland is derived from intermediate mesoderm, just like the rest of the urogenital system."
FALSE. Only the adrenal cortex is from intermediate mesoderm. The adrenal medulla is derived from neural crest (ectoderm) — it is chromaffin tissue that secretes catecholamines. Remember: "GFR = Salty, Sweet, Sexy" for the cortex; medulla = modified sympathetic ganglion.
Trap 02 — Nephron vs Collecting
"The ureteric bud gives rise to the glomerulus, Bowman's capsule, and proximal convoluted tubule."
FALSE. Reversed! The metanephric blastema (mesoderm) → NEPHRON (Bowman's capsule, PCT, LOH, DCT). The ureteric bud (outgrowth of the mesonephric duct) → COLLECTING system (collecting duct, calyces, renal pelvis, ureter). They meet via reciprocal induction.
Trap 03 — Horseshoe Kidney
"A horseshoe kidney is usually found in the pelvis because the IMA (inferior mesenteric artery) prevents its ascent."
FALSE (partly). A horseshoe kidney is typically NOT in the pelvis — it's stuck at a lower-than-normal position (around L3–L4) because the IMA blocks further ascent. A pelvic kidney (a different anomaly) is an unascended unilateral kidney that stays in the pelvis. Don't conflate the two. Horseshoe is also linked with Turner syndrome.
Trap 04 — Müllerian Ducts
"In the default (female) pathway, the Müllerian ducts regress and the Wolffian ducts form the upper 2/3 of the vagina."
FALSE. Completely reversed. In females (no MIS, no testosterone), the Wolffian ducts regress and the Müllerian ducts persist → fallopian tubes, uterus, cervix, upper 2/3 of vagina. Lower 1/3 of vagina comes from the urogenital sinus (endoderm).
Trap 05 — DHT Targets
"Testosterone (not DHT) is responsible for masculinization of the external genitalia in utero."
FALSE. DHT (not testosterone) masculinizes the external genitalia (penis, scrotum, prostate). Testosterone maintains the internal male ducts (Wolffian derivatives — epididymis, vas, seminal vesicle, ejaculatory duct). This is why 5α-reductase deficiency produces an XY with female-appearing external genitalia but normal internal male structures.
Trap 06 — CAH BP
"All forms of congenital adrenal hyperplasia (CAH) cause hypotension due to mineralocorticoid deficiency."
FALSE. BP depends on WHICH enzyme is deficient: 21-OH def (95%) → ↓aldosterone → hypotension, salt wasting; 11β-OH def → 11-deoxycorticosterone buildup (weak mineralocorticoid) → HYPERtension; 17α-OH def → ↑mineralocorticoids → HYPERtension + absence of sex hormones.
Trap 07 — Potter Sequence
"Potter sequence babies die from renal failure shortly after birth because their kidneys cannot excrete waste."
FALSE. The cause of death in Potter sequence is pulmonary hypoplasia → respiratory failure — NOT renal failure. Fetal urine makes amniotic fluid (in T2); oligohydramnios prevents lung maturation. The babies have no kidneys OR non-functioning kidneys (bilateral MCDK, PKD, obstructive uropathy), but the lungs are what fail first.
Trap 08 — Cryptorchidism Treatment
"Orchidopexy for cryptorchidism is typically delayed until after puberty to allow maximum testicular growth."
FALSE. Orchidopexy should be performed by 6 months of age (or no later than 12 months). Delay ↑ risk of infertility (heat-sensitive spermatogenesis) and seminoma/germ cell tumors. In contrast, complete AIS requires gonadectomy AFTER puberty (different indication — undescended testes produce estradiol that drives puberty).
