LO 1 · 6 Levels of Sexual Differentiation
Name the six hierarchical levels of sexual differentiation in order. Which one determines all the others?
tap to flip
Answer
1. Genetic (chromosomal) sex — XX vs XY; the master switch that drives all downstream sex.
2. Gonadal sex — ovary vs testis; controlled by SRY on Yp (makes TDF).
3. Phenotypic sex — internal ducts + external genitalia.
4. Germ-cell sex — oocyte vs spermatocyte fate.
5. Hormonal sex — estrogen/progesterone vs testosterone/DHT profile.
6. Behavioral/psychological sex — brain sexual dimorphism.

Genetic sex sits at the top and determines gonadal sex, which then drives phenotypic and hormonal sex.
LO 1 · Default vs Induced Pathway
Female development is often called the "default" pathway. What exactly is defaulted, and which signaling events actively drive male development?
tap to flip
Answer
Without SRY / TDF, bipotential gonads → ovaries by default. Without AMH, paramesonephric (Müllerian) ducts persist → uterus, fallopian tubes, upper vagina. Without testosterone, mesonephric (Wolffian) ducts regress.

Male induction requires:
• SRY → testis differentiation
• Sertoli cells → AMH → regression of Müllerian ducts
• Leydig cells → testosterone → Wolffian duct retention
• Peripheral 5α-reductase → DHT → virilization of external genitalia & prostate
LO 2 · Testosterone vs DHT vs Estrogen
Which hormone is required for each: Wolffian duct maintenance, external genital virilization, feminization of external genitalia, and bone epiphyseal fusion in both sexes?
tap to flip
Answer
Wolffian ducts (epididymis, vas deferens, seminal vesicles) → testosterone.
External male genitalia (penis, scrotum, prostate) → DHT (via 5α-reductase).
External female genitalia → develop in absence of DHT (not actively "feminized" by estrogen).
Epiphyseal closure in both sexes → estrogen (even in males, T is aromatized to E2).

Mnemonic — "Testosterone = internal, DHT = external (DICK)": DHT drives Differentiation of external genitalia, the prostate, and post-pubertal hair/acne/balding.
LO 3 · HPG Pipeline Order
Trace the HPG axis from hypothalamus to target tissue in a female. Which hormone is pulsatile and which is tonic?
tap to flip
Answer
Hypothalamus → pulsatile GnRH (every 60–90 min) → median eminence → portal system →
Anterior pituitary gonadotropes → LH & FSH (tonic levels with cycle-dependent surges) →
Ovary → estradiol (granulosa, from thecal androgens) & progesterone (corpus luteum) → target tissues.

KEY: Pulsatile GnRH = physiologic. Continuous GnRH (e.g., leuprolide given continuously) → gonadotrope desensitization → ↓LH/FSH → chemical castration.
LO 3 · Two-Cell, Two-Gonadotropin Model
Which ovarian cell has which gonadotropin receptor, and which enzyme does each cell provide in estrogen synthesis?
tap to flip
Answer
Theca cell — LH receptor → cholesterol → androstenedione / testosterone (has desmolase, 17α-hydroxylase, 17,20-lyase; lacks aromatase).
Granulosa cell — FSH receptor → aromatizes thecal androgens → estradiol (has aromatase; lacks 17α-hydroxylase).

Androgens must cross from theca → granulosa to be aromatized. At the LH surge, granulosa cells also express LH receptors → shift to progesterone production (luteinization).
LO 3 · Feedback Switch
Estradiol normally gives negative feedback, but once a month it gives positive feedback. What are the two conditions for the switch?
tap to flip
Answer
The switch to positive feedback requires:
1. E2 concentration > ~200 pg/mL (high threshold)
2. Sustained for > ~48 hours (duration)

When both are met, estradiol stimulates GnRH/LH release → the LH surge → ovulation. At all other times (low or transiently high E2), E2 inhibits GnRH, LH, FSH.

Progesterone always gives negative feedback — there is no positive feedback phase for progesterone. This is why the luteal phase shuts down gonadotropins.
LO 4 · Follicle Pool Across Life
How many oocytes does a female have at 20 weeks gestation, at birth, at puberty, and at menopause? When does meiosis I arrest occur and when does it resume?
tap to flip
Answer
Peak: 20 wk gestation ≈ 6–7 million oogonia.
Birth ≈ 1–2 million primary oocytes (all arrested in prophase I).
Puberty ≈ 300–400,000.
Menopause ≈ <1000 (functionally 0).

Meiosis I arrest occurs in fetal life at prophase I (diplotene). It resumes at the LH surge just before ovulation (polar body extruded).
Meiosis II arrest occurs at metaphase II after ovulation. Only fertilization completes meiosis II.
LO 4 · Follicle Stages in Order
Starting from primordial follicle, name the 5 stages of follicular development. Which one is FSH-independent and which is FSH-dependent?
tap to flip
Answer
1. Primordial follicle — single layer flat squamous granulosa, arrested since fetal life. FSH-independent.
2. Primary follicle — cuboidal granulosa; zona pellucida appears. FSH-independent.
3. Secondary (preantral) follicle — multi-layer granulosa + theca layers form. Starts to become FSH-responsive.
4. Antral (Graafian) follicle — fluid-filled antrum; estradiol-secreting. FSH-dependent.
5. Preovulatory / dominant follicle — one follicle wins; LH surge → ovulation.

Post-ovulation → corpus luteum → corpus albicans if no pregnancy.
LO 4 · Corpus Luteum
What cells form the corpus luteum, what hormones does it secrete, and what rescues it if pregnancy occurs?
tap to flip
Answer
After ovulation, granulosa-lutein cells (from granulosa) and theca-lutein cells (from theca interna) luteinize under continued LH stimulation.

CL secretes progesterone (dominant), estradiol, and inhibin A.

Lifespan without pregnancy = ~14 days → regresses → corpus albicans.

Rescue: if fertilization → implantation day ~7 → trophoblast hCG (LH-like) binds CL LH-receptors → CL maintained. By ~8–10 weeks, placenta takes over progesterone production (luteal-placental shift).
LO 5 · 3 Phases of the Cycle
Name the three ovarian phases and the two endometrial phases of the 28-day cycle. Which day is ovulation and which hormone drives each phase?
tap to flip
Answer
Ovarian:
• Follicular (days 1–13) — FSH selects dominant follicle; granulosa-driven estradiol rises.
• Ovulation (day 14) — LH surge ~36 h before ovulation.
• Luteal (days 15–28) — CL dominates; progesterone peaks day 21–22.

Endometrial:
• Proliferative (follicular) — estradiol thickens endometrium.
• Secretory (luteal) — progesterone induces glandular tortuosity & spiral arteries.

Luteal phase is fixed at ~14 days; cycle length varies due to follicular phase.
LO 5 · Ovulation Signals
At ovulation, what body-temperature change occurs, what cervical mucus change is classic, and which hormone underlies each?
tap to flip
Answer
Basal body temperature — rises ~0.5 °C after ovulation (progesterone is thermogenic). Sustained elevation > 16–18 days suggests pregnancy.

Cervical mucus:
• Pre-ovulatory (estrogen-dominant) — thin, clear, stretchy (spinnbarkeit); ferns when dried; pro-sperm.
• Post-ovulatory (progesterone-dominant) — thick, tacky, sperm-hostile.

LH surge can be detected in urine (OPK strips) ~24–36 h before ovulation.
Peak fertility = days 12–14.
LO 5 · Estrogen vs Progesterone Effects
Compare estradiol and progesterone effects on: endometrium, cervical mucus, breast, body temp, and uterine smooth muscle.
tap to flip
Answer
Estradiol: proliferates endometrium · thin/stretchy mucus · breast duct growth · no temp change · increases myometrial excitability (↑oxytocin receptors).

Progesterone: secretory transformation · thick/tacky mucus · breast alveolar/lobular growth · ↑BBT 0.5 °C · quiets myometrium (maintains pregnancy) · thickens/stabilizes endometrium.

Withdrawal of progesterone at the end of luteal phase → endometrial sloughing → menses.
LO 6 · Primary vs Secondary Hypogonadism
How do you distinguish primary (ovarian) from secondary (central) hypogonadism using FSH/LH vs estradiol?
tap to flip
Answer
Primary (hypergonadotropic hypogonadism): ovary fails → ↓E2 → loss of negative feedback → ↑↑FSH, ↑LH. Examples: Turner (45,XO), premature ovarian insufficiency, menopause.

Secondary (hypogonadotropic hypogonadism): hypothalamus/pituitary fails → ↓GnRH or ↓FSH/LH → ↓E2 with low or normal FSH/LH. Examples: Kallmann syndrome (anosmia + ↓GnRH), hypothalamic amenorrhea (stress/athletes/eating disorders), hyperprolactinemia, pituitary adenoma.

Rule: Problem is wherever the hormone isn't.
LO 6 · Turner Syndrome
A 14 y/o with short stature, webbed neck, and no breast development has never menstruated. What is the karyotype, the ovarian histology, and the hormone profile?
tap to flip
Answer
Karyotype: 45,XO (or mosaic 45,XO/46,XX).
Ovaries: streak gonads — accelerated follicular atresia in fetal life; no functional oocytes.
Hormones: ↓↓E2, ↑↑FSH/LH (primary hypogonadism).
Clinical: primary amenorrhea · short stature (no SHOX) · webbed neck/lymphedema · shield chest · wide-set nipples · bicuspid aortic valve · coarctation · horseshoe kidney.

Treatment: estrogen replacement at puberty; pregnancy possible only with donor oocyte.
LO 6 · Androgen Insensitivity Syndrome
A 46,XY patient is raised female, presents at puberty with primary amenorrhea, breast development, but absent pubic/axillary hair. Testosterone is elevated. Diagnosis and hormonal explanation?
tap to flip
Answer
Complete Androgen Insensitivity Syndrome (CAIS) — X-linked mutation in the androgen receptor (AR).

Mechanism: 46,XY with functional testes → make testosterone + AMH. AMH regresses Müllerian ducts → no uterus/upper vagina. Testosterone cannot signal → Wolffian ducts regress → external genitalia default to female. Peripheral aromatization of T → E2 → breast development at puberty.

Labs: ↑↑T, ↑LH, normal/↑E2. Testes often in inguinal canal — remove after puberty (malignancy risk).
Absent pubic/axillary hair = classic clue (needs androgen signaling).
LO 6 · PCOS Hormone Pattern
A 22 y/o with oligomenorrhea, hirsutism, and acne has BMI 32. List the Rotterdam criteria and the classic hormonal/metabolic abnormalities.
tap to flip
Answer
Rotterdam criteria — need 2 of 3:
1. Oligo/anovulation
2. Clinical or biochemical hyperandrogenism
3. Polycystic ovaries on ultrasound (≥12 follicles 2–9 mm, "string of pearls")

Hormonal: ↑LH, normal/↓FSH → LH:FSH ratio > 2–3:1; ↑testosterone, ↑androstenedione, ↓SHBG, ↑ estrone (aromatization in adipose).

Metabolic: insulin resistance → hyperinsulinemia → stimulates theca androgen production + ↓SHBG. Associated with obesity, dyslipidemia, endometrial hyperplasia (unopposed estrogen from anovulation).
Tx: OCPs · metformin · letrozole (for fertility) · spironolactone (hirsutism).

Step 1 traps. Read each statement carefully — each one reverses or distorts a common misconception. Choose TRUE or FALSE.
Trap 01 · LO 1 — SRY Source
"The SRY gene is located on the long arm of the Y chromosome (Yq)."
FALSE. SRY is on the short arm of Y (Yp11.3). Translocation of SRY to an X chromosome during aberrant meiosis causes 46,XX sex-reversed males (de la Chapelle syndrome). Remember: Short arm → SRY.
Trap 02 · LO 2 — "Female is Active Estrogen-Driven"
"Normal female external genitalia development requires estradiol signaling from the fetal ovary."
FALSE. Female external genitalia develop in the absence of androgens, not because of estrogens. This is why 46,XY CAIS (AR mutation) patients still have normal female-appearing external genitalia despite XY karyotype. Fetal estrogen is not required; the default pathway is female.
Trap 03 · LO 3 — Continuous vs Pulsatile GnRH
"Continuous GnRH administration would be expected to stimulate LH/FSH release and induce ovulation."
FALSE. GnRH must be pulsatile (every ~60–90 min). Continuous GnRH (e.g., leuprolide depot) desensitizes gonadotropes and suppresses LH/FSH — used as chemical castration in prostate cancer, endometriosis, and central precocious puberty. Pulsatile leuprolide, by contrast, induces ovulation.
Trap 04 · LO 3 — Aromatase Location
"Theca cells express aromatase and directly synthesize estradiol from cholesterol."
FALSE. Granulosa cells have aromatase (FSH-driven). Theca cells have desmolase + 17α-hydroxylase + 17,20-lyase (LH-driven) and supply androstenedione to granulosa. This is the two-cell, two-gonadotropin model. At the LH surge, granulosa acquire LH receptors and luteinize.
Trap 05 · LO 3 — Progesterone Feedback
"Sustained high progesterone during the luteal phase exerts positive feedback that primes the next LH surge."
FALSE. Progesterone exerts only negative feedback on the HPG axis. High luteal progesterone suppresses GnRH pulse frequency, which is exactly why another dominant follicle can't mature during the luteal phase — and why continuous-progestin contraception prevents ovulation. Only estradiol switches to positive feedback.
Trap 06 · LO 4 — Oocyte Meiosis Completion
"The secondary oocyte released at ovulation has already completed meiosis II."
FALSE. At ovulation, the oocyte has completed meiosis I (first polar body extruded) and is arrested in metaphase II. Meiosis II only completes when sperm penetrates (fertilization) — this is when the second polar body forms. An unfertilized oocyte degenerates in metaphase II.
Trap 07 · LO 5 — Luteal Phase Length
"In irregular cycles, variation in cycle length is due to variation in the luteal phase."
FALSE. The luteal phase is fixed at ~14 days (corpus luteum lifespan). Variability in cycle length comes from the follicular phase. That's why you count backwards from the next expected menses to estimate ovulation (e.g., a 35-day cycle → ovulation day 21, not day 14).
Trap 08 · LO 6 — CAIS and Pubic Hair
"A 17-year-old with complete androgen insensitivity syndrome presents with well-developed pubic and axillary hair despite primary amenorrhea."
FALSE. In CAIS, androgen receptors don't work, so pubic and axillary hair are absent or scant. Breast development is present (from peripheral T → E2 aromatization). Classic triad: 46,XY · primary amenorrhea · breast dev but absent sexual hair · blind-ended vagina, no uterus.
