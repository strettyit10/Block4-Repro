Testis — 3 Layers
Name the three coverings of the testis from outermost to innermost. Which layer is the thickest?
tap to flip
Answer
1. Visceral tunica vaginalis (outermost) — single-cell mesothelial layer of cuboidal cells; derived from the processus vaginalis.

2. Tunica albuginea (middle) — THICKEST layer; dense connective tissue. Sends septa inward to divide testis into lobules.

3. Tunica vasculosa (innermost) — loose CT with rich vasculature; supplies nutrients + carries hormones.
Pampiniform Plexus & Torsion
What is the pampiniform plexus and why is it important? What clinical emergency arises when it twists?
tap to flip
Answer
Pampiniform plexus = web of veins wrapping around the testicular artery. Acts as a countercurrent heat exchanger to cool arterial blood before it reaches the testis (spermatogenesis needs ~2°C below core body temp).

Testicular torsion: twisting of spermatic cord → cuts off arterial supply → ischemia. Surgical emergency — must be untwisted within 6 hours to save the testis. Presentation: acute severe unilateral scrotal pain, high-riding testis, absent cremasteric reflex, "bell-clapper" deformity.

Varicocele: dilation of pampiniform plexus ("bag of worms"), more common on left (left gonadal vein → left renal vein at 90°).
Leydig Cells
Where are Leydig cells located? What do they produce and under what stimulation? Histologic markers?
tap to flip
Answer
Location: In the interstitium BETWEEN seminiferous tubules (not inside the tubules).

Products: Testosterone (main) + a small amount of DHT. Stimulated by LH from anterior pituitary.

Testosterone maintains: spermatogenesis, accessory glands (prostate, seminal vesicles, bulbourethral), male secondary sex characteristics, libido.

Histo markers: Lipid droplets, smooth ER, mitochondria with tubular cristae, Reinke crystals (pathognomonic).

DHT = 50× more potent than testosterone (binds androgen receptor with higher affinity).
Sertoli Cells & Blood-Testis Barrier
Where are Sertoli cells located? What forms the blood-testis barrier and why is it essential?
tap to flip
Answer
Location: INSIDE the seminiferous tubules, resting on the basement membrane. Stimulated by FSH.

Sertoli-Sertoli tight junctions form the blood-testis barrier, dividing tubules into:
• Basal compartment (near BM): spermatogonia
• Adluminal compartment (near lumen): meiotic germ cells

Why it matters:
1. Separates immune-privileged meiotic cells from circulation (meiosis begins post-puberty — cells are "foreign" to immune system).
2. Meiotic cells rely on Sertoli cells for nutrients.

Also: secrete inhibin B (neg feedback on FSH), MIS/AMH (fetal), ABP (concentrates testosterone).
Spermatogenesis — Cell Progression
Trace the germ cell progression from base to lumen of the seminiferous tubule. Which step is the only mitotic division?
tap to flip
Answer
Germ cells move from basement membrane → lumen as they mature:

1. Spermatogonia (2n) — divide by MITOSIS (only mitotic step) — sit on basement membrane.
2. Primary spermatocytes (4n) — enter meiosis I.
3. Secondary spermatocytes (2n) — complete meiosis II.
4. Spermatids (n) — begin spermiogenesis (morphologic transformation).
5. Spermatozoa (n) — mature sperm released into lumen.

Process takes ~74 days. Synchronous divisions via incomplete cytokinesis (cytoplasmic bridges) ensure continuous sperm supply.
HPG Axis (Male)
Trace the hypothalamic-pituitary-gonadal axis in males. Which hormone targets Leydig vs. Sertoli cells? Where does each feedback signal act?
tap to flip
Answer
Hypothalamus: GnRH → ant. pituitary
Ant. pituitary (basophils of pars distalis):
• LH → Leydig cells → testosterone ("LH → Leydig")
• FSH → Sertoli cells → support germ cells + ABP + inhibin B ("FSH → Follicle/Sertoli")

Feedback:
• Testosterone → negative feedback on hypothalamus (GnRH) AND pituitary (LH)
• Inhibin B → selective negative feedback on FSH only

Clinically: ↑FSH + normal T = Sertoli dysfunction (↓ inhibin B); ↑LH + ↓T = Leydig dysfunction.
Spermiogenesis
What is spermiogenesis (vs. spermatogenesis)? Name the four parts of the mature sperm structure.
tap to flip
Answer
Spermiogenesis = final morphologic transformation of round spermatid → elongated spermatozoon (subset of spermatogenesis). No cell division.

Key events: acrosome forms (hydrolytic enzymes for zona pellucida penetration), nucleus condenses, flagellum (axoneme) forms, mitochondria coil around midpiece, residual cytoplasm discarded.

Mature sperm structure:
• Head: nucleus + acrosome
• Mid-piece: axoneme (9+2 microtubules) + mitochondrial sheath (ATP for flagellar motility)
• Principal piece: axoneme + fibrous sheath
• End piece: axoneme + plasma membrane only
Excurrent Ducts — Order
Trace the path of sperm from seminiferous tubule to urethra. Which two ducts are intratesticular?
tap to flip
Answer
Sperm pathway: Seminiferous tubule → Tubulus rectus → Rete testis → Efferent ductules → Epididymis (head→body→tail) → Vas (ductus) deferens → Ampulla → Ejaculatory duct → Prostatic urethra → Membranous urethra → Penile urethra

Mnemonic: SEVEN UP — Seminiferous tubule, Epididymis, Vas deferens, Ejaculatory duct, Nothing, Urethra, Penis.

Intratesticular: Tubulus rectus + Rete testis.
Extratesticular: everything else starting with efferent ductules.
Efferent Ductules
What is the embryologic origin of the efferent ductules? What cell types line them? Function?
tap to flip
Answer
Origin: Mesonephric tubules (from pronephric/mesonephric system).

Structure: ~20 small tubules leaving rete testis → coalesce into head of epididymis. Give the duct a characteristic "scalloped/uneven lumen" on histology.

Cell types:
• Ciliated cells → move sperm forward
• Resorptive (non-ciliated) cells → reabsorb fluid → concentrate sperm

Key concept: mesonephric tubules → fluid reabsorption = CONCENTRATE sperm as they move toward epididymis.
Epididymis
What is the epithelium of the epididymis? What are the three parts and what happens in each?
tap to flip
Answer
Epithelium: Pseudostratified columnar with STEREOCILIA (long, non-motile microvilli — not true cilia). Also has smooth muscle in wall.

Three parts (head → tail ~7 m long!):
• Head: immature sperm enter from efferent ductules; cannot yet fertilize.
• Body: transit + continued maturation.
• Tail: sperm fully mature → motility + fertilizing ability.

Storage: Lumen widens toward tail → slowed flow → sperm can be stored up to 1 month.

Embryology: Wolffian (mesonephric) duct derivative.
Vas Deferens & Vasectomy
What epithelium lines the vas deferens? What are the wall's distinguishing features? What is vasectomy?
tap to flip
Answer
Epithelium: Pseudostratified columnar with stereocilia (same as epididymis).

Wall: 3 THICK smooth muscle layers (inner longitudinal, middle circular, outer longitudinal) — provide powerful peristaltic propulsion during ejaculation. This is how to distinguish vas from epididymis histologically (vas has much thicker muscular wall).

Vasectomy: surgical cut/ligation of vas deferens → prevents sperm from reaching ejaculate. Sperm are still made in testis but reabsorbed. Testosterone and ejaculate volume unaffected (sperm are <5% of semen volume).

Embryology: Wolffian duct (SEED).
Seminal Vesicles
What do seminal vesicles secrete? What percentage of ejaculate do they contribute? Are sperm stored here?
tap to flip
Answer
Histo: Simple or pseudostratified columnar epithelium; highly folded mucosa for ↑ surface area.

Secretion (~70% of ejaculate):
• Fructose (energy for sperm — clinical marker of SV patency)
• Alkaline fluid (neutralizes acidic vagina)
• Prostaglandins (stimulate uterine contractions)
• Clotting proteins (semenogelin)
• Vitamin C, flavins (give semen yellow UV fluorescence — forensic use)

SPERM ARE NOT STORED HERE — common exam trap! Sperm are stored in the tail of the epididymis. SV fluid joins sperm at the ejaculatory duct.
Prostate
What lines the prostate? What are corpora amylacea? What is PSA and its clinical use?
tap to flip
Answer
Histo: Simple or pseudostratified columnar epithelium. Unique to mammals. Contains the verumontanum — landmark where ejaculatory duct meets prostatic urethra.

Corpora amylacea = small, round calcified bodies in prostatic glandular lumens. Classic histologic feature of the prostate. Increase with age.

PSA (prostate-specific antigen): serine protease secreted by prostatic epithelium. Liquefies semen coagulum after ejaculation. Leaks into blood when prostatic architecture disturbed → ↑ serum PSA = marker of BPH, prostatitis, or prostate cancer.

Clinical: Prostate cancer is the most common cancer in men (3rd deadliest). Hormone-sensitive → treated with androgen deprivation therapy.
Bulbourethral (Cowper's) Glands
Where are Cowper's glands located, what do they secrete, and what is the distinguishing histologic clue?
tap to flip
Answer
Location: At the base of the penis; secrete into the proximal penile (spongy) urethra. Paired glands.

Secretion: Alkaline mucin (pre-ejaculate fluid) — two roles: (1) protects urethra from urine acidity; (2) shields sperm from damaging enzymes (residual acrosomal proteases).

Histologic clue: the gland is surrounded by SKELETAL MUSCLE — the only accessory gland like this — because it sits within the urogenital diaphragm (external urethral sphincter).

Female homolog: Bartholin (greater vestibular) glands.
Penis — Erectile Tissues
Name the paired and unpaired erectile tissues of the penis. Which one surrounds the urethra? What covers them?
tap to flip
Answer
Corpus cavernosum — PAIRED dorsal erectile bodies; fill with blood during erection, surrounded by tunica albuginea (dense CT). Compression against tunica during engorgement causes rigidity.

Corpus spongiosum — UNPAIRED ventral body; SURROUNDS the urethra. Expands distally as the glans penis. Less rigid during erection (doesn't compress urethra — allows ejaculation).

Penis parts: Root (fixed, attached to body wall) · Body (shaft, with dartos muscle) · Glans (expanded tip) · Prepuce (foreskin) covers glans.

Erection: Parasympathetic (NO, "Point") → vasodilation → engorgement. Ejaculation: sympathetic (NE, "Shoot").
Urethra — 3 Segments
Name the three segments of the male urethra and the epithelium of each.
tap to flip
Answer
Three segments distinguished by epithelium:

1. Prostatic urethra — passes through the prostate; receives ejaculatory ducts + prostatic ducts. Lined by transitional (urothelium) epithelium (same as bladder). Contains verumontanum.

2. Membranous urethra — shortest; passes through urogenital diaphragm (external urethral sphincter — skeletal muscle, voluntary). Lined by pseudostratified/stratified columnar epithelium.

3. Penile (spongy) urethra — longest; runs through corpus spongiosum. Lined by pseudostratified/stratified columnar, transitions to stratified squamous at the glans (navicular fossa).

Key: transitional (prostatic) → columnar (membranous + penile) → squamous (glans).

Test Trap Mode: Each statement is either TRUE or FALSE as written. Many are designed to catch common exam errors. Click T or F — then read the explanation carefully.
Trap 01 — Leydig vs Sertoli Location
"Leydig cells are located inside the seminiferous tubules, while Sertoli cells sit in the interstitium between tubules."
FALSE. Reversed. Sertoli cells are INSIDE the seminiferous tubules (on the basement membrane) and surround developing germ cells. Leydig cells are in the INTERSTITIUM between tubules. Mnemonic: "LEydig = in Exterior; Sertoli = Sitting among sperm." LH → Leydig (→ testosterone); FSH → Sertoli (→ ABP, inhibin B, germ cell support).
Trap 02 — Which Germ Cell Does Mitosis?
"Primary spermatocytes undergo mitosis to maintain the germ cell pool throughout life."
FALSE. Spermatogonia (not spermatocytes) are the ONLY germ cells that undergo mitosis. They are the stem cells on the basement membrane, dividing into Type A (self-renewal) + Type B (committed) daughters. Primary spermatocytes undergo MEIOSIS I. Secondary spermatocytes undergo MEIOSIS II. Spermatids undergo spermiogenesis (no division). Memorize: gonia = mitosis; cytes = meiosis; tids = morphologic shaping.
Trap 03 — Blood-Testis Barrier
"The blood-testis barrier is formed by tight junctions between Leydig cells, separating spermatogonia from immune-sensitive cells."
FALSE. The blood-testis barrier is formed by tight junctions between SERTOLI cells (not Leydig). It divides the seminiferous tubule into the basal compartment (spermatogonia) and adluminal compartment (meiotic spermatocytes + spermatids). The barrier protects MEIOTIC and post-meiotic cells — which express novel antigens — from circulating immune cells. Damage (e.g., trauma, vasectomy reversal) can cause anti-sperm antibodies → immunologic infertility.
Trap 04 — Seminal Vesicle Storage
"Seminal vesicles are the primary storage site of mature sperm before ejaculation."
FALSE. SPERM ARE NOT STORED IN SEMINAL VESICLES — classic exam trap! The seminal vesicles produce fluid (fructose, prostaglandins, ~70% of ejaculate volume) that joins sperm at the ejaculatory duct. Sperm are stored in the TAIL of the epididymis (up to ~1 month). The name "seminal vesicle" is misleading — it refers to their secretory role in semen, not storage.
Trap 05 — Pampiniform Plexus
"The pampiniform plexus is an arterial network that warms blood flowing to the testis to optimize sperm production."
FALSE. Two errors: it's a VENOUS plexus (not arterial), and it COOLS (not warms) blood. The pampiniform plexus is a web of veins wrapping around the testicular artery, functioning as a countercurrent heat exchanger to keep testicular temperature ~2°C below core body. Spermatogenesis is heat-sensitive. Clinically: dilation of the plexus = varicocele ("bag of worms," usually left-sided) → infertility. Twisting = testicular torsion, a surgical emergency.
Trap 06 — Cilia vs Stereocilia
"The pseudostratified epithelium of the epididymis has motile cilia that propel sperm toward the vas deferens."
FALSE. The epididymis (and vas deferens) have STEREOCILIA, NOT true motile cilia. Stereocilia are long, non-motile microvilli — purely structural (↑ surface area for fluid reabsorption, sperm maturation). Sperm are propelled by smooth muscle contractions of the ductal wall. True motile cilia exist in the efferent ductules and rete testis — but those use dynein-driven 9+2 microtubules for actual movement. Don't confuse the two: stereo = structure, cilia = motion.
Trap 07 — Bulbourethral Surroundings
"The bulbourethral (Cowper's) glands are surrounded by smooth muscle, distinguishing them from other accessory glands."
FALSE. The bulbourethral glands are surrounded by SKELETAL muscle, not smooth muscle — this is their histologic pathognomonic feature. They sit within the urogenital diaphragm (external urethral sphincter = skeletal). Function: secrete alkaline mucin (pre-ejaculate) that (1) neutralizes residual urine acidity in the urethra and (2) protects sperm from damaging acrosomal enzymes. Female homolog: Bartholin (greater vestibular) glands.
Trap 08 — DHT Potency
"Testosterone binds the androgen receptor more potently than DHT — that's why testosterone is the dominant circulating androgen."
FALSE. Reversed. DHT binds the androgen receptor with ~50× higher affinity than testosterone (DHT is the more potent androgen). Testosterone is the main CIRCULATING androgen (higher serum levels) because most tissues convert it to DHT locally via 5α-reductase. Drugs like finasteride (5α-reductase inhibitor) reduce DHT production specifically — used for BPH and androgenetic alopecia. Defective androgen receptor = Complete Androgen Insensitivity Syndrome (CAIS).
