LO 1
ON EXAM — 6-TIER CASCADE: Chromosomal → Gonadal → Hormonal → Morphological → Secondary sex → Brain/behavior. DEFAULT is FEMALE at every tier — male anatomy needs active testis-secreted hormones (T + AMH). SRY (not the Y chromosome itself) determines sex → SOX9 → Sertoli commitment → testis. Sertoli FIRST (AMH), Leydig SECOND (T). Estrogen closes epiphyses in BOTH sexes. Male brain masculinization is actually estradiol (T aromatized locally; α-fetoprotein protects female fetus). Karyotype variants: Turner (45,X), Klinefelter (47,XXY), de la Chapelle (46,XX SRY+ → male), Swyer (46,XY SRY− → female)

LO 3
ON EXAM — Pulsatile GnRH only (continuous = shutdown, basis of leuprolide depot). Two-cell/two-gonadotropin model: LH→THECA→androgens; FSH→GRANULOSA→aromatase→estradiol. Granulosa LACKS 17α-hydroxylase — can't finish estradiol alone. Feedback: moderate E2 = NEGATIVE; sustained E2 >200 pg/mL x 48 h = POSITIVE → LH surge (AVPV kisspeptin). Progesterone BLOCKS positive feedback (no second surge in luteal phase). Kisspeptin: AVPV (positive) + arcuate (negative) — AVPV apoptoses in males. LH/FSH/TSH/hCG share α-subunit → hCG can trigger ovulation and cross-stimulate TSHR

LO 4
ON EXAM — Oocytes arrest PROPHASE I from fetal life → LH SURGE completes meiosis I → arrest METAPHASE II → fertilization completes meiosis II. Stage parade: Primordial → Primary → Secondary → Antral → Graafian → CL → Albicans. Theca has LH-R from secondary stage; granulosa has FSH-R throughout + LH-R LATE (Graafian) — this enables surge to trigger ovulation. CL hormones: PEI (Progesterone, Estradiol, Inhibin A). LO 5
ON EXAM — Day 1 = 1st day of bleeding. Ovulation day 14 (~36 h after LH begins rising). Luteal phase = FIXED 14 days; cycle variability lives in FOLLICULAR phase. Ovarian cycle (follicular→ovulation→luteal) drives uterine cycle (menses→proliferative→secretory). Day-21 progesterone >5–10 ng/mL confirms ovulation. BBT rises 0.3–0.5°C after ovulation (progesterone thermogenic). Estrogen = GROW (endometrium, breasts); Progesterone = PROTECT (thickens cervical mucus, quiets myometrium, builds glycogen). Unopposed estrogen → endometrial hyperplasia/cancer

10 vignettes weighted across LOs.
Q1. A 16-year-old phenotypically female patient presents with primary amenorrhea. Exam shows normal breast development, sparse axillary/pubic hair, and a blind-ending vagina. Karyotype is 46,XY. Bilateral inguinal masses are palpable. Labs: elevated testosterone, elevated LH, elevated estradiol. No uterus on imaging. Which BEST explains this presentation?
A. Swyer syndrome (SRY mutation)
B. 5α-reductase type 2 deficiency
C. Complete androgen insensitivity syndrome (CAIS) — X-linked AR loss-of-function
D. Persistent Müllerian duct syndrome
E. 21-hydroxylase deficiency
C — Complete AIS.
46,XY with functional Sertoli cells makes AMH → Müllerian regression (no uterus, blind vagina). Leydig cells make T but AR is non-functional → Wolffian regresses, external defaults female, no pubic/axillary hair (AR-dependent). Peripheral aromatase converts circulating T → E2 → ERα drives breast development. AR on the X chromosome → X-linked recessive inheritance. Testes are usually in the inguinal canal.
Q2. A 3-day-old 46,XX infant presents with ambiguous genitalia (clitoromegaly, partial labial fusion), vomiting, hypotension, hyponatremia, and hyperkalemia. Newborn screen returns elevated 17-hydroxyprogesterone. The MOST likely diagnosis is:
A. Turner syndrome
B. 21-hydroxylase deficiency CAH
C. 5α-reductase deficiency
D. Complete AIS
E. Aromatase deficiency
B — 21-OH deficiency CAH.
Can't make cortisol or aldosterone → substrate shunts to androgens (DHEA, androstenedione, T) in a 46,XX fetus → in-utero virilization of external genitalia. Normal uterus and ovaries (gonadal T not required). Aldosterone deficiency → salt-wasting crisis (hypotension, hyponatremia, hyperkalemia). Elevated 17-OH-progesterone is diagnostic. Medical emergency — IV hydrocortisone + fluids + electrolyte correction + later fludrocortisone.
Q3. A 46,XY infant is born with female-appearing external genitalia. Internal exam reveals normal testes, vasa deferentia, and epididymides, but no uterus. At puberty, the patient develops phallic growth, muscle mass, and deepening voice. Labs: normal testosterone, LOW DHT, elevated testosterone-to-DHT ratio. Enzyme deficient?
A. 17,20-lyase
B. Aromatase
C. 5α-reductase type 2
D. 21-hydroxylase
E. 17β-HSD3
C — 5α-reductase type 2 deficiency.
5α-RD2 converts T → DHT in peripheral tissues (genital skin, prostate, hair follicles). Without DHT, external genital masculinization fails in utero → female-appearing external at birth. Wolffian ducts (T-dependent, not DHT) develop normally → normal internal male. At PUBERTY, rising testosterone partially overcomes the DHT block → late virilization (“guevedoces” — “penis at 12”). Labs: normal T, LOW DHT, elevated T:DHT ratio.
Q4. In the ovarian two-cell / two-gonadotropin model, which statement is CORRECT?
A. Thecal cells express aromatase and produce estradiol
B. Granulosa cells express 17α-hydroxylase and produce androgens
C. LH stimulates thecal cells to produce androstenedione; FSH stimulates granulosa cells, which aromatize androstenedione to estrone → 17β-HSD1 → estradiol
D. FSH stimulates theca; LH stimulates granulosa
E. Both cells make estradiol independently
C — LH/theca & FSH/granulosa.
Thecal cells have 17α-hydroxylase + 17,20-lyase → produce androstenedione under LH. They LACK aromatase. Granulosa cells have FSH-driven aromatase (CYP19A1) but lack 17α-hydroxylase. Androstenedione diffuses from theca to granulosa → aromatized to estrone → 17β-HSD1 converts to 17β-estradiol. Mnemonic: LAT (LH-Androgens-Theca) / FAG (FSH-Aromatase-Granulosa).
Q5. During the normal menstrual cycle, sustained elevation of estradiol above ~200 pg/mL for approximately 48 hours triggers an LH surge. Which hypothalamic population mediates this positive feedback, and why can't adult males mount an LH surge?
A. Arcuate kisspeptin; males have apoptosed arcuate neurons
B. AVPV kisspeptin (positive feedback); in males, perinatal androgens are aromatized to estradiol locally, which triggers APOPTOSIS of AVPV kisspeptin neurons
C. Paraventricular GnRH; males lack PVN neurons
D. SON vasopressin; suppressed in males
E. No hypothalamic mediator — surge is pituitary-intrinsic
B — AVPV kisspeptin; apoptosed perinatally in males.
Two kisspeptin populations: AVPV (anteroventral periventricular, within the POA) mediates POSITIVE feedback; sustained high E2 activates it → GnRH burst → LH surge. Arcuate kisspeptin mediates NEGATIVE feedback and tonic pulses. In males, perinatal androgens are locally aromatized to estradiol in the hypothalamus → triggers apoptosis of AVPV kisspeptin neurons. Arcuate population preserved. Males retain negative feedback but cannot produce a surge. This is the “aromatase paradox.”
Q6. A 29-year-old woman with normal cycles until 3 months ago presents with oligomenorrhea, hirsutism, acne, and a BMI of 34. Labs: LH:FSH ratio 2.8, elevated free testosterone, mildly elevated DHEA-S, and transvaginal ultrasound shows bilateral “string of pearls” follicles. Which mechanism BEST explains her elevated FREE testosterone despite normal total?
A. Exogenous anabolic steroids
B. Adrenal androgen-secreting tumor
C. Hyperinsulinemia and obesity suppress hepatic SHBG synthesis → raise free T
D. 21-hydroxylase deficiency
E. Pregnancy
C — Hyperinsulinemia suppresses SHBG.
PCOS hyperandrogenism has two drivers: (1) ovarian thecal overproduction of androgens (LH:FSH > 2 + insulin co-stimulation of theca); (2) hyperinsulinemia + obesity SUPPRESS hepatic SHBG synthesis → higher free testosterone fraction for the same total. Metformin and weight loss raise SHBG and reduce clinical hyperandrogenism. Classic Rotterdam criteria met: oligomenorrhea + hyperandrogenism + polycystic ovaries.
Q7. A 22-year-old elite distance runner with BMI 17 presents with secondary amenorrhea of 10 months duration, frequent stress fractures, and low bone mineral density. Labs: LH 1.2, FSH 2.8, estradiol 18 pg/mL. Prolactin and TSH normal. Pregnancy test negative. MOST likely diagnosis and mechanism?
A. Primary ovarian insufficiency (POI)
B. Prolactinoma
C. Functional hypothalamic amenorrhea (FHA) — low leptin → low kisspeptin → low GnRH
D. Polycystic ovary syndrome
E. Kallmann syndrome
C — FHA (female athlete triad).
Low energy availability (BMI 17 + excessive training) → low leptin → reduced kisspeptin drive → reduced GnRH pulse frequency/amplitude → Lo-Lo pattern (low LH/FSH + low E2). Female athlete triad = low energy availability + menstrual dysfunction + low bone mineral density. POI would show HIGH FSH/LH; prolactinoma would elevate prolactin; PCOS would have high LH + androgens; Kallmann would include anosmia and congenital delayed puberty. Treat with behavioral intervention first (restore energy + reduce training); HRT/OCP for bone if persists.
Q8. A 37-year-old woman presents with 9 months of secondary amenorrhea, hot flashes, and vaginal dryness. Labs: FSH 68, LH 42, estradiol 12 pg/mL, normal TSH, normal prolactin, negative pregnancy test. Karyotype shows 45,X/46,XX mosaicism. Fragile X premutation positive. What is the diagnosis and mechanism?
A. Menopause (too young to matter)
B. Primary ovarian insufficiency (POI) — accelerated follicular atresia
C. Kallmann syndrome
D. Turner syndrome classic (45,X)
E. Complete AIS
B — Primary Ovarian Insufficiency.
POI = menopausal labs (FSH > 40) + amenorrhea before age 40. Causes include Fragile X premutation, Turner mosaic, autoimmune oophoritis, chemo/radiation, galactosemia, idiopathic. Hi-Lo pattern: loss of ovarian inhibin + estradiol → negative feedback gone → FSH/LH rise. Treatment: HRT until average age of menopause (~51) to protect bone + cardiovascular health. Fertility counseling: spontaneous conception rare but possible. Work up karyotype, fragile X, autoimmune screen.
Q9. A healthy 29-year-old woman undergoes endometrial biopsy on day 22 of her menstrual cycle for evaluation of infertility. The specimen shows tortuous sacculated glands, glycogen-rich cells, and subnuclear vacuoles with decidualized stroma. The dominant hormone driving this appearance is:
A. Estradiol
B. FSH
C. Progesterone from the corpus luteum
D. Prolactin
E. hCG
C — Progesterone from the corpus luteum.
Day 22 = mid-luteal phase. The secretory phase endometrium (glandular, tortuous, glycogen-rich, decidualized stroma) is driven by progesterone from the CL. Subnuclear vacuoles classically appear days 16–18 (“early secretory”). This biopsy finding CONFIRMS that the patient ovulated (she has a functioning CL). Endometrial biopsy is one way to confirm ovulation in an infertility workup; serum day-21 progesterone >5-10 ng/mL is the standard test.
Q10. A 58-year-old postmenopausal woman with an intact uterus is considering HRT for persistent vasomotor symptoms. Her physician recommends combined estrogen-progestin rather than estrogen alone. The BEST reason is:
A. Progestin amplifies estrogen's vasomotor relief
B. Progestin prevents estrogen-induced VTE
C. Progestin opposes estrogen's proliferative effect on the ENDOMETRIUM — reducing endometrial hyperplasia and adenocarcinoma risk
D. Progestin is required for bone preservation
E. Estrogen alone causes hot flashes
C — Progestin protects the endometrium.
Unopposed estrogen drives endometrial proliferation → hyperplasia → adenocarcinoma. In a woman with a uterus, HRT MUST include a progestin. Post-hysterectomy patients can use estrogen alone. Timing hypothesis: HRT risk-benefit most favorable when started <60 years old or within 10 years of menopause. VTE and breast CA risks are intrinsic to estrogen (not mitigated by progestin). Same principle underlies endometrial cancer risk in PCOS (unopposed low-level estrone from adipose aromatase without progesterone).
